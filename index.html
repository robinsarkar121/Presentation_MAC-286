Part 1 · The problem

Fear and price usually move apart

The VIX is Wall Street’s “fear gauge”. On most days, when the S&P 500 rises, fear falls, and when it falls, fear rises. We gave every day a signal:

Part 2 · Our method

Counting streaks and scoring each day

Example: ten days of signals

How we score each signal day

Profit % = (later close − signal-day close) ÷ signal-day close × 100
  • “Later close” = the S&P 500 close 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 trading days later
  • Profit above 0 is a winner; anything else is a loser

One statistics object

RECORDED EVERY TIME
  • Number of trades
  • Number of winners and losers
  • Total profit %
  • Total profit for winners and for losers
COMPUTED AT THE END
  • % winners = winners ÷ trades × 100
  • Average profit = total profit ÷ trades
  • Average winner and average loser

One object for each pattern, streak length and number of days ahead.

Part 4 · Conclusion

Conclusion

What 29 years of S&P 500 and VIX data tell us

Both UP is a warning sign

When stocks and fear rise together, the S&P 500 does worse than normal for the next 1–5 days. Random days almost never do this badly.

Both DOWN is only mildly positive

Slightly better than an ordinary day, but random days do as well about 15% of the time, so it could be luck.

Long streaks are too rare

3 in a row happened only 8 and 6 times in 29 years, about what chance predicts. Too few to judge.

A warning, not a strategy

The difference is a few tenths of a percent per trade, before trading costs: a risk signal, not a money machine.
